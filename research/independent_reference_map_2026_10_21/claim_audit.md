# Claim audit — proposal statements against accessible evidence (v2 enrichment)

This audit preserves useful project hypotheses while separating them from evidence. “Narrowed” means the original statement is too broad given sources inspected; it does not establish the opposite claim.

| Proposal statement | Evidence checked | Audit outcome | v1 treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| “No modeling framework currently couples 1D PDE transport with transient surface kinetics for acetylene semi-hydrogenation” (main.tex §2, line 164) | Urmès et al. 2022 abstract and first-page preview describe periodic acetylene hydrogenation over Pd/α-Al₂O₃, a single-site microkinetic transient model, parameter estimation and reproduction of fixed-bed outlet composition for multiple amplitudes/frequencies. Full article/SI unavailable. | **Broad claim is narrowed.** A directly relevant transient acetylene model exists. The accessible sources do not establish whether it uses a spatially resolved axial PDE or optimizes cycle-integrated ethylene yield, nor whether it covers PdAg. | Claim a PdAg-specific spatial-model/optimization gap only as an open question until full text/SI are checked. |
| Fast temperature pulsing yielded up to 40-fold enhancement and 20–30 μs response, offered as support for concentration pulsing (main.tex §1.1, lines 118–120; §2, line 158). | Zhu et al. 2021 abstract: heater response up to 230 °C within 20–30 μs; model platform and CO oxidation on Pt. | **Not transferable as acetylene evidence.** It demonstrates a different thermal stimulus, reaction and catalyst. The abstract's heater response is not a measured acetylene coverage time. | Keep only as a separate apparatus precedent; exclude from BEP kinetic constants or forcing rationale. |
| Conventional acetylene kinetic models rely on PSSA, so rapid forcing necessarily breaks it (main.tex §2, line 160). | PdAg 2012, Pd 2019 and periodic Pd 2022 abstracts reveal model-specific kinetics; 2022 directly estimates transient parameters. Poto et al. 2022 citation in the proposal literature text concerns methanol synthesis over Cu/CeO₂/ZrO₂. | **Overgeneralized and partly mis-cited.** PSSA is not universal; a methanol paper is not direct acetylene evidence. Whether PSSA is acceptable depends on target-system timescales and data. | Treat dynamic coverages as a testable model choice; compare reduced and dynamic models. |
| “Internal coverages in a full-scale reactor are physically impossible to observe” (main.tex §2, line 162). | Urakawa et al. 2008 full text describes MES detection of dynamic interfacial species using in-situ spectroscopy and phase-sensitive analysis, in a different lab catalytic example. | **Absolute wording is too strong.** MES can observe selected dynamic species in suitable experimental systems. This does not imply full-bed, spatially resolved industrial coverage measurement. | Distinguish boundary-only industrial sensing from lab-scale operando spectroscopy. |
| Dynamic concentration operation requires “zero physical reactor redesign” (main.tex §1.1–1.2, lines 120, 124). | Proposal itself later plans MFC modulation; Silveston et al. 1995 review notes laboratory forcing uses reactant/diluent flow control. No apparatus spec was inspected. | **Unsupported as written.** Reactor vessel may remain, but mixing, actuators, control and safety qualification may be needed. | Require a feasible-input and hardware model; leave actual redesign need open. |
| Forcing must satisfy τ_desorption ≈ T_pulse < τ_residence (main.tex §3.1, line 197). | Proposal's own stated hypothesis; 2022 Pd periodic abstract reports frequency response but does not establish a universal law. | **Project hypothesis, not established general condition.** Multiple surface and transport modes can produce a wider response. | Use as a candidate search range only after target-catalyst timescales are measured. |
| Square waves cause “severe numerical instabilities”; Fourier “struggles with nonlinear frequency mixing” (main.tex §3.4, line 260). | Urakawa et al. 2008 full text p. 4907 notes square-wave stimulation is practical with on/off valves. Numerical treatment depends on event segmentation; Fourier truncation of jumps yields Gibbs oscillations. | **Categorical rejection is unsupported.** Square input is a manageable discontinuity with suitable solver handling; Fourier is a representation with truncation tradeoffs. | Compare square, spline and Fourier controls under identical bandwidth and integration checks. |
| Gradient-based descent “inevitably” converges to a local minimum, sufficient for a yield-maximization (main.tex §3.4, line 263). | Mathematical interpretation of the stated optimization direction and local-search scope. | **Wrongly categorical and objective-sign mismatch.** Local methods may converge to a local maximum, minimum of a negated objective, boundary point or stationary solution; no global guarantee. | Report solver termination, feasibility, multiple starts and objective spread. |
| A microsecond-versus-seconds timescale gap makes the PDE-to-ODE system stiff (main.tex §3.2, line 208). | The cited microsecond evidence is Zhu's heater response on Pt/CO oxidation; no target PdAg transient constants were available. | **Plausible numerical concern, not yet measured for BEP.** Stiffness depends on calibrated model Jacobian and solver behavior. | Run eigenvalue/time-scale and solver-work diagnostics after calibration. |
| The compact gas PDE's reaction term is physically unambiguous (main.tex §3.1, Eq. at lines 182–185). | The source does not state in that equation whether (u) is superficial/interstitial, (r_i) is a signed species source or reaction extent, or all density units. | **Needs a declared basis.** A stoichiometric source and bed-volume balance clarify porosity and catalyst mass conversion. | v1 states a conservative convention and treats it as a derivation, not a verbatim equation. |
| The main 1D PDE represents pellet-scale transport (main proposal vs. roadmap). | main.tex §3.1 gives a bed-scale source term only; roadmap separately adds a particle diffusion PDE; Shi et al. 2024 abstract reports particle-resolved reaction–diffusion modeling. | **Not in core baseline.** Adding pellet diffusion silently would change model scope. | Keep pellet/film transport as a supporting extension and test limitation criteria for target pellets. |
| The proposal's three-phase calibration/optimization/physical test establishes expected performance. | main.tex §§3.4–3.5 describes future work; no parameter set, run result or experimental data were supplied. | **Planned work only.** | No performance gain, optimized waveform or validation is claimed in v1. |

## Source-access qualifications

The Urakawa MES paper was read in full. Most catalyst kinetics were reviewed only through abstracts or indexed metadata. The 2022 article's exact spatial model scope remains unresolved because its full article and Supporting Information were not accessible. The cited 2024 thesis was not located in the checked repository Articles directory or web search. These limitations are represented in `sources.yaml` and `uncertainties.md`.


## Second-pass audit — important claim boundaries

The v1 table is retained as the audit trail. The following additions reflect the second-pass sources and derivations; each is conditional and remains AI-authored.

| Claim or inference under review | Second-pass evidence / reasoning | Outcome and map treatment |
|---|---|---|
| “No acetylene periodic model exists.” | Urmès et al. (2022) publisher abstract explicitly reports small-amplitude periodic flow experiments on Pd/α-Al₂O₃, dynamic parameter estimation and outlet-composition prediction in a fixed bed. Full methods/SI were not accessible here. | **Falsified in its broad form.** Whether the full model is a distributed axial PDE, and whether it optimizes performance against steady operation, remain unverified. |
| Surface memory, lag, or a “resonance” window is enough to imply a performance gain. | The LTI mean relation and static-concavity Jensen bound exclude gain in their respective model classes. Feimer et al. (1982) abstract reports no rate gain for its tested adsorption/desorption models; Ziegelhoeffer et al. (1972) abstract describes a model-specific pellet selectivity gain. | **Not sufficient.** Dynamic modes may change response; nonlinear rectification’s sign depends on mechanism, amplitudes, constraints and objective. |
| Any nonlinearity yields a changed mean under periodic operation. | Espinoza et al. (2022) report a model with a catalyst oxidation-state descriptor yet no mean performance difference across the simulated amplitude/period/phase sweep in its studied ethane-ODH regime; the authors describe the tested response as effectively linear. | **Not generally true.** This is a cross-system model-specific null result, not proof about acetylene/PdAg. |
| Periodic operation never improves, or always improves, catalytic performance. | The above null examples contrast with the sufficient-condition literature and the Ziegelhoeffer pellet example; Kaps et al. (2026) report experimental FPO improvement for a different methanol CSTR with paired input modulation and deactivation complications. | **Neither universal conclusion is supported.** Transfer depends on the target model, experimental regime, metric and constraints. |
| A conventional steady point is enough to prove a periodic gain. | Set inclusion gives `J*_periodic ≥ J*_steady` only when all steady controls embed as constants in the periodic feasible set and objective/plant/constraints are identical. | **Benchmark requirement tightened.** Use an appropriately optimized steady comparison; weak mathematical ordering does not imply strict gain or real-world attainability. |
| A single ethylene selectivity average is an unambiguous performance score. | Cycle-integrated conversion, selectivity and net yield have distinct denominators. A ratio of integrated amounts is not generally the average of instantaneous ratios; inlet ethylene must be subtracted. | **Operational metric still open.** Define carbon closure, feed basis, conversion floor and productivity denominator before comparison. |
| The C₂H₂→C₂H₄→C₂H₆ path fixes one surface mechanism, and side-product exclusions transfer across catalysts. | Urmès et al. (2019) full text reports no detected C₄ products only for its specific Pd/α-Al₂O₃ conditions; it selected a one-site model. Pachulski et al. (2012) abstract selects a two-site PdAg/Al₂O₃ model; Li et al. (2021) study Pd₁Ag₃/TiO₂ working-surface structure; Mei et al. (2009) use idealized-surface KMC. | **Catalyst-specific alternatives retained.** Stoichiometric bookkeeping, fitted mechanism, non-detection limit and ideal-surface model are distinguished. |
| Outlet phase lag directly identifies catalyst-coverage memory. | Espinoza et al. (2022) demonstrate apparatus-specific validation of MFC response, inert flow compensation, time/spatial profiling, and temperature/structure measurement in another fixed-bed chemistry. | **Not identifiable from outlet phase alone.** Bed RTD, mixer, actuator, transfer line, analyzer and sample schedule must be separated from surface kinetics. |
| Pellet diffusion and thermal dynamics are already part of the proposal’s 1-D baseline. | The main proposal’s bed source does not resolve a radial pellet concentration or thermal field. The full-text profile-reactor study measured local temperature but its apparatus and reaction differ. | **Remain optional model extensions.** Equations are labeled derived templates; target transport/thermal measurements are absent. |
| A dynamic model or fit establishes an operating gain. | Urmès et al. (2022) address kinetic estimation/prediction; Kaps et al. (2026) and Espinoza et al. (2022) ask/answer different performance questions in other chemistries. | **Evidence tasks separated.** Identification, prediction, model discrimination and matched performance validation are not interchangeable. |

### Source-access and scope notes added in v2

The TU Hamburg repository provides the full Espinoza et al. (2022) article; its detailed result is retained with the specific ethane-ODH chemistry, model sweep, and limited experiment scope. Kaps et al. (2026) full publisher HTML was inspected and the source record’s author list corrected against the article. The publisher page for Urmès et al. (2022) returns the abstract and a listed SI file, but this session could not retrieve article methods/SI. Accordingly the distributed-model and performance-comparison questions remain open. Feimer, Horn–Bailey, Ziegelhoeffer, Mei, Li and Pachulski claims remain at abstract/metadata access as logged in `sources.yaml`.
