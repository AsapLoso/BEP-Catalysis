## The seven-step supported-Pd example (Urmès et al., 2019)

Urmès et al. model 0.05 wt% Pd/α-Al₂O₃ under tail-end conditions using a Horiuti–Polanyi-type mechanism. In their **Table 3**, with `*` denoting an empty effective site:

```text
1   H₂(g) + 2*           ⇌ 2H*                 hydrogen splits on the surface
2   C₂H₂(g) + *          ⇌ C₂H₂*              acetylene adsorbs
3   C₂H₂* + H*           ⇌ C₂H₃* + *          first H addition → vinyl-like intermediate
4   C₂H₃* + H*           ⇌ C₂H₄* + *          second H addition → adsorbed ethylene
5   C₂H₄*                ⇌ C₂H₄(g) + *        ethylene desorbs (desired product)
6   C₂H₄* + H*           ⇌ C₂H₅* + *          third H addition → ethyl intermediate
7   C₂H₅* + H*           ⇌ C₂H₆(g) + 2*       fourth H addition → ethane released
```

The equations above preserve the **reversible reaction arrows in Table 3** as the elementary model notation, not their later kinetic reduction assumptions. Urmès et al. subsequently designate particular second-H-addition steps as rate determining and others as quasi-equilibrated **for that model and dataset**. Those rate-law assumptions are a separate kinetic subject, not automatically applicable to the BEP.

### How that relates to R1, R2 and R3

- **R1, acetylene → ethylene:** acetylene adsorbs, receives H twice, and bound ethylene desorbs. R1 is not merely the single step `C₂H₂* → C₂H₄*`; it includes hydrogen activation and ethylene release.
- **R2, ethylene → ethane:** ethylene already present in the gas (feed or previously released product) can adsorb by the reverse of step 5, then receives H twice more via `C₂H₅*`. *Pre-existing gas ethylene is therefore also a reactant.*
- **R3, acetylene → ethane overall:** bound acetylene can receive **all four H additions before ethylene desorbs**. This gives an acetylene-to-ethane product without the intermediate ethylene ever becoming gaseous. Its **net equation equals R1 + R2**, but that fact neither proves nor disproves a separately identifiable rate.

### A genuinely different intermediate sequence in a different source

Mei, Neurock and Smith (2009) use first-principles-based kinetic Monte Carlo models for Pd(111) and Pd–Ag/Pd(111) **model surfaces**. Their study describes:
- a selective **vinyl → ethylene** route;
- a competing **vinyl → ethylidene → ethane** route (along with dehydrogenation toward ethylidyne, which can block sites);
- Ag addition suppressing the unselective ethylidene route **within their investigated surface models**, with altered hydrogen/ethylene desorption and selectivity.

**Ethylidene is not the same surface intermediate as adsorbed ethylene**, even if both have chemical formula `C₂H₄`. The alternative route is **not** verified for the BEP catalyst, and its depiction does not authorize an independent third rate constant to be fitted.

## The role of adsorption — deliberately a separate Tier-5 subject

Before H can be added, species need a source of surface hydrogen and reactants must encounter the appropriate catalytic environment. **Both adsorption and the elementary surface reaction mechanism** matter, but are distinct questions. In the Urmès model hydrogen dissociates (`H₂ + 2* ⇌ 2H*`), acetylene occupies an effective site (`C₂H₂ + * ⇌ C₂H₂*`), and later products can desorb.

The **number of surface sites occupied by a molecule is model dependent**: one *effective* site in a Langmuir-type description does not mean every molecule necessarily contacts only one Pd atom or that geometry/size can always be neglected. Adsorption orientation may matter, but electronic bonding and transition-state stabilization are also fundamental catalytic effects.

## Catalyst/model dependence — prominent warning

This branch describes **possible mechanisms in different published models, not an established mechanism for our BEP catalyst**. Sources differ in metal (Pd versus PdAg), support (α-Al₂O₃ versus BEP material), surface ensemble, feed, temperature, and measured/assumed kinetics. A one-site mechanism used in 2019 and two-site kinetic description reported in the 2012 PdAg/Al₂O₃ abstract can both be reasonable effective models without being interchangeable or independently resolving atomic-scale geometry.

**Do not infer:** (i) which microscopic paths dominate our reactor; (ii) that R3 is an independent identified rate; (iii) that only one or both reactants must adsorb for *all* catalysts; (iv) verified BEP adsorption energies/coverages; or (v) a numerical effect of periodic input.
