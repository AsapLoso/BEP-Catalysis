## Main question — the owner's actual scientific uncertainty

**Can a gas-phase reactant penetrate through the pellet's pore network and react directly with acetylene already adsorbed on internal active sites, or does our supported-Pd/PdAg kinetic picture instead assume that both reaction partners adsorb first?**

This combines **two separate issues to disentangle**, rather than declaring them equivalent:

1. **Transport/accessibility:** Do relevant molecules reach internal pore surfaces at the experiment's flow, temperature and timescales? Surface deep inside a porous pellet is not categorically inaccessible. The **effectiveness factor/reaction–diffusion** question is about transport relative to consumption; it is not itself a claim about Eley–Rideal chemistry.
2. **Microscopic encounter:** After a gas molecule reaches an active surface, must that species first adsorb (a Langmuir–Hinshelwood-like route), or can it react directly with an already adsorbed species (Eley–Rideal-like)? The chemical mechanism determines which description is justified. The Urmès 2019 example has dissociated adsorbed hydrogen, but that does not uniquely identify our PdAg catalyst.

**Related model question:** The simple one-model-site-per-adsorbate picture is not atomistic geometry. How do we count available sites, and does dissociative H₂ adsorption require nearby vacancies in the actual model?

**Another important check before deeper study:** Are the particular **Urmès 2019 supported-Pd mechanism** and **Mei 2009 Pd/PdAg model-surface pathway alternatives** relevant sources for *this* experiment? Would the owner be making a significant scientific mistake by learning these models as a basis for the BEP, even with their caveats?
