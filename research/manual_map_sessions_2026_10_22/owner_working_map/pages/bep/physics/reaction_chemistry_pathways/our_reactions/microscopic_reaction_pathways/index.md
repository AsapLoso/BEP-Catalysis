# Microscopic Reaction Pathways

Our three net reactions say **what** can be produced. Here we're trying to understand **how** the reactants may turn into products on the catalyst.

One possible sequence, described in a supported-palladium model by [Urmès et al. (2019), Table 3](https://doi.org/10.3390/catal9020180), starts with acetylene binding to the surface. Hydrogen attaches in steps:

```text
C₂H₂* → C₂H₃* → C₂H₄*
                     ├─→ C₂H₄(g)  (ethylene leaves)
                     └─→ C₂H₅* → C₂H₆(g)  (more hydrogenation)
```

Here * means a surface-bound species. Ethylene might leave as the product we want, or remain on the surface and be further hydrogenated. Ethylene that was already in the gas can also adsorb and react.

Another published model, [Mei et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2009.09.004), considers a different intermediate route through **ethylidene**. These are models for particular catalysts and surfaces, not proof that either pathway dominates in our reactor. The detailed elementary equations and the two ChatGPT-generated diagrams are retained as **AI references**, not confirmed chemistry.
