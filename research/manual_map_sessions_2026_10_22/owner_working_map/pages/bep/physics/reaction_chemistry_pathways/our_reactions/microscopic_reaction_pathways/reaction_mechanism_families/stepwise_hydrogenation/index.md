## Owner's understanding (23 October 2026, edited paraphrase)

Acetylene reacts on the catalyst by successively taking on one additional hydrogen atom at a time. After enough additions it can become ethylene. Ethylene can leave the surface; if it stays and takes on more hydrogen, ethane can be formed instead.

The owner suggested that these steps might form radicals and could make the environment very reactive. **This is an open hypothesis**, *not* an owner-approved explanation or a human-verified source claim. Ask Damian which intermediates have radical character, if any, in the actual model.

## AI scientific qualification (not part of the human approval)

In a supported-Pd Horiuti–Polanyi-type model, H₂ first dissociates to adsorbed H; then surface-bound acetylene can proceed via vinyl and bound ethylene, with a desorption/continued-hydrogenation fork. A star marks an adsorbed surface species, **not a free radical**. Surface bonding and electronic structure matter, so neither an isolated radical mechanism nor a high radical concentration follows from the steps. These are source-model interpretations, not confirmed BEP PdAg elementary steps or rates.

**Source:** [Urmès et al., *Catalysts* 2019, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.3390/catal9020180), supported Pd/α-Al₂O₃. AI read of full text; user/Damian source verification pending.
